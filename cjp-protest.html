"Jantar Mantar 2.0" — Youth-led movement demands CEC resignation & SIR rollback

📌 Executive Summary

On October 10, 2026, the youth-led satirical movement Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) staged its second major protest at New Delhi's Jantar Mantar — dubbed "Jantar Mantar 2.0" — despite the Delhi Police denying permission. The demonstration demanded the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and a rollback of the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

Ahead of the protest, Delhi was placed under an unprecedented security lockdown — metro stations shut, hundreds of trains cancelled, internet suspended, and over 35,000 security personnel deployed. Multiple detentions were reported in the pre-dawn hours, including YouTuber Dhruv Rathee, detained from his Gurugram residence.

🎯 The Three Core Demands

The CJP's demands were publicly articulated in the lead-up to the protest:

  1. Resignation of CEC Gyanesh Kumar over alleged irregularities in the electoral revision process.
  2. Immediate freeze of the SIR exercise and restoration of the January 2025 electoral roll.
  3. Repeal of the Election Commissioners appointment law and an independent, transparent selection procedure.

Source: Times of India (25 Sept 2026), ABP Live (24 Sept 2026).

🔒 Delhi Under Siege

Ahead of the protest, authorities implemented sweeping security measures across the national capital:

Security deployment: Over 35,000 Delhi Police and paramilitary personnel were deployed across the capital, with over 30 blockade points at city borders. (Sources: Indian Express / New Indian Express, 10 Oct 2026)

Metro restrictions: Entry and exit gates at 45 metro stations (initially proposed 57, later reduced to 45) were closed from 9 PM on October 9 until 6 PM on October 10. Closed stations included Janpath, Patel Chowk, Central Secretariat, Rajiv Chowk, Mandi House, Kashmere Gate, Hauz Khas, and Supreme Court. (Sources: The Hindu, 9 Oct 2026; Times Now, 10 Oct 2026)

Train cancellations: Over 250 trains were cancelled or rescheduled, including 179 local services and 75+ long-distance express and mail trains. Services to and from New Delhi and Hazrat Nizamuddin stations were severely affected. (Source: Times of India live blog, 10 Oct 2026)

Internet suspension: Mobile internet services were suspended within a 4 km radius of the Janpath-Kartavya Path crossing from 10 PM on October 9 until 10 PM on October 10. (Source: Times of India, 10 Oct 2026)

Permission denied: The Delhi Police denied permission for the protest, citing late submission of the application (October 7, only 3 days before), non-compliance during the June-July agitation, and public safety concerns. Joint Commissioner of Police Nupur Prasad stated that prohibitory orders under Section 163 of the BNSS were in force in the New Delhi area. (Sources: The Hindu, 9 Oct 2026; News On Air, 10 Oct 2026)

The Union Home Ministry, through Home Secretary Govind Mohan, directed all states and UTs to coordinate with Delhi Police and prevent the movement of protesters toward Delhi. (Source: ThePrint / New Indian Express, 9-10 Oct 2026)

🎥 Dhruv Rathee Detained

YouTuber and political commentator Dhruv Rathee was detained by Gurugram Police from his residence in Kendriya Vihar society, Sector 56, Gurugram, at approximately 7:10 AM IST on October 10, 2026.

According to Hindustan Times, Rathee was detained after refusing to comply with police instructions not to travel to Delhi for the CJP protest. A senior Gurugram Police official had earlier stated: "In case he denies and tries to travel to Delhi, he will be detained."

Rathee had posted an Instagram reel informing his followers that he would be travelling to Delhi for the protest. The CJP responded on X: "Dhruv Rathee has been detained. The Dictator is TERRIFIED of young people who question its wrongdoings and refuse to stay silent!"

Sources: Hindustan Times, Times of India, News18, The Tribune (all 10 Oct 2026).

🚔 Mass Detentions Reported

Delhi Police detained multiple protesters in the pre-dawn hours and early morning of October 10. Reported figures varied across sources:

  • 150 protesters detained on Friday, October 9, per Telegraph India and Times Now.
  • Over 200 detained across the New Delhi area on the morning of October 10, per News18.
  • 600+ people with criminal records also held in preventive operations, per Times Now.
  • At least 100 individuals with criminal records suspected of joining the protest detained overnight, per The Hindu.

CJP and SFI (Students' Federation of India) claimed that thousands of protesters were detained across states — from trains, buses, and hotels — to prevent them from reaching Delhi. Maharashtra Police allegedly detained 22 AISF protesters en route to Delhi. (Source: ThePrint, 9 Oct 2026)

Detained individuals were shifted to six designated centres: Chhatrasal Stadium, Rajiv Gandhi Stadium, Yamuna Sports Complex, Surajmal Stadium, Community Centre Daulatpur, and Community Centre Pandrawal. (Source: The Week, 10 Oct 2026)

🔥 The July Context

This is not the CJP's first major confrontation with authorities. In June–July 2026, during the movement's first major protest over the NEET-UG 2026 paper leak, a 36-day sit-in at Jantar Mantar ended with the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan on July 25, 2026.
